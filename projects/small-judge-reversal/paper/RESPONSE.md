# Response to REFEREE round 1: small-judge-reversal paper

*Produced by an autonomous AI agent (Claude) on behalf of @pranavnandyalam. Not peer reviewed.*

The full referee report text was not in the repository when this revision was made. The points below follow the numbering
used in `RESULTS.md` ("fix 1-4", "optional fix 7") and the Lead's summary of the report. Points 6, 8, 9 and 10 are matched to
the remaining requested changes by topic; that mapping is our inference and may not match the referee's numbering.
All numbers are from `results/calibrated.md/.json` (`src/calibrated.py`), `results/analysis.md/.json` and, for the round-2 scale check, `paper/figures/paper_stats.json` (`paper/make_figures.py`). No new model runs were made.

**1. The "position-locking, not criterion-blindness" interpretation is a readout artifact. Conceded.**
We added a letter-calibrated readout d = (s_cf - s_rf)/2 per question. Pearson r(d_better, d_W1) is 0.95, 0.63, 0.90, 0.63
(all CIs well above 0) and the same-sign share is 0.92, 0.71, 0.87, 0.76. The order-averaged logit shift is therefore strongly *positively*
correlated under "better" and "worse": the judges are criterion-blind (see round 2 below for what d does and does not measure), and position-locking was the argmax view of a large letter offset.
The title, abstract, introduction, contributions, results, discussion and conclusion were rewritten around this. New Table III
(corr, same sign, calibrated accuracy, calibrated reversal, subsets, letter null) and new Fig. 2 (scatter of d_better vs d_W1,
generated by `paper/make_figures.py`, r values cross-checked against `results/calibrated.json`). The calibrated "reversal"
shares (0.06-0.22) are reported, but we say explicitly that no null was defined for them and we do not read them as evidence of reversal.

**2. H2 is near-tautological against a uniform null. Conceded.**
An independent-letter judge with each model's observed per-question A-rates gives all four judgments the same letter in
1.00/0.63/0.79/0.34 of pairs (observed 1.00/0.71/0.89/0.43). Expected position-consistent accuracy p(1-p) is 0.000/0.159/0.053/0.224
(observed 0.000/0.103/0.050/0.430). The paper now says that H2 is supported as pre-registered but is nearly implied by letter marginals,
so it is uninformative about the criterion (Results, paragraph "H2 is close to tautological"; Threats (v)).

**3. Sample composition (HumanEvalPack-heavy). Conceded.**
217/300 pairs are HumanEvalPack code pairs (correct vs buggy code, often near-identical text; we did not quantify textual overlap).
The paper states this in the abstract, Setup ("Composition caveat"), Table III and Scope. Non-HEP subset (n=83): r = 0.95, 0.72, 0.91, 0.57;
HEP (n=217): 0.96, 0.58, 0.91, 0.69. Calibrated "better" accuracy is lower on non-HEP pairs (0.43/0.53/0.48/0.61 vs 0.68/0.48/0.61/0.88).

**4. Readout reliability. Partly done.**
Done: restricting to pairs with A/B mass > 0.9 on all four passes (n = 300/288/286/140) gives r = 0.95/0.60/0.91/0.63.
Not done: a generation-based check of the logit readout. This is stated in Results (Robustness iii), Threats (iii) and "What was not done".

**5. Claims too broad. Conceded.**
Title narrowed to "four sub-2B Qwen judges" and "a code-heavy RewardBench subsample". Abstract, Scope and Conclusion restrict the claim to
these four judges, the argmax A/B logit readout (and its order-averaged difference), thinking off and this subsample.

**6 (topic: related work / positioning). Done.** Abstracts fetched on 2026-10-06 and cited only for what they state:
- Liu et al., arXiv:2410.02205 (negation invariance as part of logical preference consistency, REPAIR). The paper now says our test is
  an instance of negation invariance and that our contribution is narrow (order swaps, sub-2B judges, calibrated readout).
- Elkins & Chun, arXiv:2601.21433 (negation sensitivity of ethical stance; small 1-4B models swing up to 76 pp). Cited as related,
  with the caveat that it is stance-taking, not response comparison.
- Norman, Rivera & Hughes, arXiv:2606.19544 (21 judges, nine providers; reliability coexisting with position bias in two deployed judges).
  Its abstract does not single out small judges, so it is cited for position bias in general, not as small-judge literature.

**7. 3-4B judge and thinking-enabled condition (optional). Not done.** No new model runs in this revision (the pre-registered budget rule
excluded extension judges). Stated as a limitation in Scope, "What was not done" and the Conclusion.

**8 (topic: "50 judges" claim about arXiv:2609.32407). Checked; kept, wording clarified.** The fetched abstract says the study covers
"64 open-weight evaluators" and that on LLMBar "the verdicts of 50 judges agree with human labels only 0.456 of the time, even after
averaging both answer orders". Both numbers are in the abstract; the paper states 64 judges overall and 50 for the LLMBar figure.
We also removed the earlier wording that this work reports "small-judge" position bias, which its abstract does not say.

**9 (topic: pre-registered vs post-hoc analyses). Done.** The calibrated readout, letter-biased null and subset analyses are labelled
post hoc in Method, Table III caption and Threats (i); their CIs are described as descriptive, without multiplicity control.

**10 (topic: controls). Partly done.** The W2 and length controls are still reported with the argmax readout, and we now say they
inherit its letter-bias problem. They were not re-analysed with the calibrated readout (listed under "What was not done").

Other changes: the per-section table and the argmax taxonomy figure were removed from the paper to make room (the per-section numbers
remain in `results/analysis.md`; `make_figures.py` still writes `fig_taxonomy.pdf`). The y-axis label of Fig. 1 was clipped and is fixed.

## Round 2: overseer PAPER REJECT

All four requested changes were made. The scale numbers below come from `paper/figures/paper_stats.json` (`calibrated_check`), which `paper/make_figures.py` now writes from `results/raw_*.json`. No new model runs.

**R2-1. Qwen2.5-0.5B "distinguishes the responses". Removed.** The parenthetical "(even Qwen2.5-0.5B, which always outputs ``A'')" is gone. Results now say that for this judge d is tiny relative to the letter offset: median |d_better| = 0.047 logits vs. a mean order-averaged A-minus-B score of 2.80 (Qwen3-1.7B: median |d_better| 5.415, rounded to 5.4 in the paper). Its r = 0.95 therefore shows only that small residual shifts recur across questions. It is not evidence of a coherent preference. The same caveat is Threat (iii).

**R2-2. Headline 0.95. Done.** The abstract now says that 0.95 comes from the always-"A" judge and that the other three judges give 0.63-0.90. The conclusion gives "0.63 to 0.90 for the three judges that do not always answer A; 0.95 for Qwen2.5-0.5B" and notes that only Qwen3-1.7B shows clear content signal (0.81).

**R2-3. "Distinguish the two responses" wording. Replaced.** Abstract, introduction, contribution 3, Results and Discussion now describe d as a pair-dependent logit shift that the question mostly leaves unchanged, not as a content preference. A new Threat (iii), "What d measures", says that calibrated better-accuracy is near chance (0.50-0.61) for three judges and that only Qwen3-1.7B (0.81) shows clear content signal. The later threats are renumbered (iv)-(viii).

**R2-4. Threat (ii). Extended.** It now says that content-by-slot interactions (e.g. an A logit that depends on the length of the text in slot A) enter d identically under both questions and would push r upward without any criterion-independent preference.

The H1 paragraph says the argmax and calibrated readouts disagree on size. The trend is labelled post hoc, two sizes in each of two families, confounded with letter-bias differences. RESULTS.md H1 bullet updated likewise.

**E2. Magnitude. Done.** New Table IV: OLS slope of d_W1 on d_better 0.82/0.39/0.67/0.26, median |d_W1|/|d_better| 0.90/0.82/0.89/0.35, P_C(better)/P_C(W1), S and Delta. **E3. Length control as partial positive control. Done.** Results: r(d_length, character-length difference) = 0.12/0.68/0.60/0.46 (n = 72) with CIs, calibrated sign accuracy 0.65/0.61/0.64/0.58; but r(d_length, d_better) = 0.96/0.89/0.91/0.74 and r(d_better, length difference) = 0.06/0.50/0.50/0.34, so the text says it is not a clean control.

**E4. Edit distance. Done.** Setup now gives the HEP edit-distance quantiles (median 1 token; 131/217 at distance 1; 204/217 below 5; non-HEP median 45) and states that HEP results are about one-word bug edits. Robustness gives r and Qwen3-1.7B accuracy by bin (ed = 1, 2, >=3) and notes only 13 HEP pairs have ed >= 5. "Criterion-blind" in abstract, Discussion and Conclusion is replaced or qualified by "on this sample, for these small judges, the logit readout is dominated by a pair-dependent shift that the negated question does not invert".

**E5. Not done and caveats. Done (listed, not executed).** "What was not done (planned for a next cycle)" lists (a) generation-based readout validation (greedy decoding on >=100 pairs) and (b) a larger positive-control judge (Qwen3-4B / Qwen2.5-7B), stating that without (b) criterion-blindness cannot be separated from insensitivity of the test. Robustness (ii) notes the mass>0.9 filter keeps 140/300 pairs for Qwen3-1.7B with calibrated accuracy 0.72 vs 0.81 overall. New threat (i') reports the letter-null excess (0.71 vs 0.63, 0.89 vs 0.79, 0.43 vs 0.34) as a sign of pair-level letter offsets; threat (i) says all calibrated/null analyses are post hoc without multiplicity control and that W2 and the both-orders length shares are argmax-based.

Done; all three fetched 2026-10-07 (abstract and HTML).** Added SLMJury (Laddha, Pradhan, Srivastava, arXiv:2606.07810: 16 SLM judges 0.6-14B; verdicts parsed from generated text; no logit scoring, order swap or criterion reversal found in the part read, about 100k of 177k characters), Xie, Zhao, Chen, Cohan, Zhao (arXiv:2608.30005, Findings of EMNLP 2026 per the arXiv comment; Qwen3 0.6B-8B; Table 3, RaR-Science-Static, Qwen3-1.7B macro-F1 generative 0.443, logprob 0.449, probe 0.835), and LLM-RankFusion (Zeng et al., arXiv:2406.00231; both permutations, A/B token probabilities combined).
